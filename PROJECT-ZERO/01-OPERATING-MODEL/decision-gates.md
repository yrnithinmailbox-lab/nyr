# NYR Project Zero — Decision Gates

## 1. Purpose

Decision gates prevent weak assumptions, insufficient evidence, and uncontrolled investment from moving directly into implementation.

Every opportunity must pass appropriate gates before progressing.

## 2. Gate 01 — Signal Relevance

Question: Is this signal relevant enough to investigate?

Requirements:
- Identifiable observation.
- Traceable source.
- Relevant research division.
- Plausible connection to a problem or opportunity.

Possible outcomes:
- Accept for investigation.
- Monitor.
- Archive as irrelevant.

## 3. Gate 02 — Problem Credibility

Question: Is there sufficient evidence that a meaningful problem exists?

Requirements:
- Clear problem statement.
- Identifiable affected users.
- Supporting evidence.
- Current workarounds or alternatives.
- Initial severity and frequency assessment.

Possible outcomes:
- Accept as a research problem.
- Gather more evidence.
- Reject or archive.

## 4. Gate 03 — Opportunity Gap

Question: Is there a meaningful gap in existing solutions?

Requirements:
- Existing solution landscape.
- Competitor and alternative analysis.
- Documented limitations.
- Potential differentiation.
- Evidence of an underserved need.

Possible outcomes:
- Formulate an opportunity.
- Investigate further.
- Monitor existing market developments.
- Reject the opportunity.

## 5. Gate 04 — Opportunity Quality

Question: Is this opportunity sufficiently attractive to prioritize?

Requirements:
- Completed weighted evaluation.
- Evidence confidence grade.
- Technical feasibility considerations.
- Commercial assumptions.
- Global impact assessment.
- Risk assessment.

Possible outcomes:
- Prioritize deep validation.
- Investigate further.
- Hold.
- Reject.

A high score with low evidence confidence remains a hypothesis.

## 6. Gate 05 — Experiment Readiness

Question: Can the most important assumption be tested safely and economically?

Requirements:
- Falsifiable hypothesis.
- Defined methodology.
- Measurable success criteria.
- Failure criteria.
- Budget limit.
- Risk assessment.
- Required permissions and resources.

Possible outcomes:
- Approve experiment design.
- Revise experiment.
- Hold.
- Reject.

Experiment approval does not automatically authorize product development.

## 7. Gate 06 — Build Authorization

Question: Is there sufficient evidence and explicit approval to develop a solution?

Requirements:
- Documented validation results.
- Clear target users.
- Demonstrated problem relevance.
- Technical feasibility assessment.
- Commercial viability assessment.
- Security, privacy, legal, and operational review.
- Explicit authorization for implementation.
- Approved budget and resource limits.

Possible outcomes:
- Authorize a defined build.
- Request additional validation.
- Pivot.
- Hold.
- Reject.

No product implementation or significant expenditure is authorized automatically by passing earlier gates.

## 8. Mandatory Rejection Conditions

An opportunity must be rejected or suspended when it fundamentally depends on:

- Unauthorized access or data collection.
- Deception, manipulation, or harmful monetization.
- Unmanageable privacy or safety risks.
- Unacceptable legal or licensing restrictions.
- Unsupported assumptions that invalidate its core value.

Document the reason for rejection.

## 9. Decision Record Requirements

Every significant decision must record:

- Decision ID.
- Opportunity ID.
- Gate number.
- Outcome.
- Supporting evidence.
- Contradictory evidence.
- Rationale.
- Date.
- Reviewer.
- Follow-up actions.

## 10. Decision Governance Principle

Passing a research gate permits progression to the next approved research activity.

It does not imply automatic approval for financial investment, external commitments, or product implementation.

**Evidence earns consideration. Validation earns confidence. Explicit authorization enables execution.**
