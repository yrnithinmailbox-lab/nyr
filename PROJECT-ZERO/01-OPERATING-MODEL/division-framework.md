# NYR Project Zero — Research Division Framework

## 1. Purpose

NYR Project Zero operates through 12 interconnected research divisions.

Each division investigates a distinct area of opportunity while remaining capable of collaborating with other divisions.

The objective is to discover meaningful problems, identify opportunity gaps, and develop evidence-backed recommendations.

## 2. Division Framework

### R01 — Opportunity Intelligence

**Scope:** Discover unsolved problems, unmet customer needs, business inefficiencies, and emerging demand.

Research questions:
- What problems are repeatedly reported but poorly resolved?
- Which customer needs are underserved?
- Where do existing products fail?
- What problems are becoming more important?

Expected output:
- 15 documented problem signals per initial assignment.
- Evidence references.
- Existing solution landscape.
- Priority problem candidates.

### R02 — Collective Knowledge

**Scope:** Knowledge discovery, fragmented information, search, retrieval, and knowledge connections.

Research questions:
- Why is useful knowledge difficult to retrieve?
- Where is information fragmented across systems?
- How can disconnected knowledge become actionable?
- What knowledge remains inaccessible despite being publicly available?

Expected output:
- 10 documented knowledge gaps.
- Existing product and research comparisons.
- Potential knowledge infrastructure opportunities.

### R03 — Future Simulation

**Scope:** Forecasting, scenario planning, simulation, digital twins, and decision intelligence.

Research questions:
- Which expensive decisions could be simulated cheaply?
- Where could digital twins improve decision-making?
- What scenarios are difficult to predict?
- Where can simulation replace costly trial and error?

Expected output:
- 5 simulation opportunities.
- Potential users and use cases.
- Technical and economic feasibility considerations.

### R04 — Digital Workforce

**Scope:** AI workers, autonomous systems, collaborative agents, and workflow automation.

Research questions:
- Which workflows require excessive manual coordination?
- What tasks can be safely delegated to digital workers?
- Where do existing automation solutions fall short?
- How should humans supervise autonomous systems?

Expected output:
- 10 workflow candidates.
- Current workflow analysis.
- Automation potential and human oversight requirements.

### R05 — Self-Healing Systems

**Scope:** Diagnostics, fault detection, automated recovery, reliability, and resilient infrastructure.

Research questions:
- Why do automated systems still require manual recovery?
- Which failures occur repeatedly?
- What recovery tasks can be automated safely?
- Where is system resilience insufficient?

Expected output:
- 10 failure patterns.
- Root-cause observations.
- Existing recovery solution analysis.
- Potential resilience improvements.

### R06 — Trust Infrastructure

**Scope:** AI reliability, verification, security, governance, identity, and trust mechanisms.

Research questions:
- What prevents AI systems from being trusted in production?
- How can automated decisions be verified?
- Where are identity and authorization mechanisms insufficient?
- What controls are missing from autonomous systems?

Expected output:
- 10 documented reliability or trust gaps.
- Existing standards and solutions.
- Potential trust infrastructure opportunities.

### R07 — New Revenue Systems

**Scope:** New business models, recurring revenue opportunities, underserved markets, and digital commerce.

Research questions:
- Which underserved needs could support recurring revenue?
- What valuable services lack sustainable business models?
- Which industries have inefficient monetization structures?
- Where could new distribution models create value?

Expected output:
- 10 business model hypotheses.
- Potential customer segments.
- Revenue mechanisms.
- Initial commercial assumptions.

### R08 — Resource Intelligence

**Scope:** Optimization of time, money, energy, inventory, infrastructure, and other resources.

Research questions:
- Where are businesses wasting measurable resources?
- Which inefficiencies create recurring costs?
- Can unused capacity become economically useful?
- Where could better forecasting reduce waste?

Expected output:
- 10 inefficiency patterns.
- Potential cost savings.
- Measurement opportunities.
- Possible optimization solutions.

### R09 — Alternative Marketplaces

**Scope:** Exchange of services, skills, knowledge, unused capacity, and underutilized assets.

Research questions:
- What valuable resources are difficult to discover or exchange?
- Where do existing marketplaces exclude participants?
- What prevents efficient matching of supply and demand?
- Could new exchange mechanisms unlock unused value?

Expected output:
- 5 marketplace hypotheses.
- Supply and demand analysis.
- Trust and transaction considerations.

### R10 — Planet Intelligence

**Scope:** Environmental challenges, water, energy, waste, climate adaptation, and sustainability.

Research questions:
- Which environmental problems have affordable technology gaps?
- Where is environmental data insufficient?
- What waste streams could become useful resources?
- Which local environmental problems have scalable solutions?

Expected output:
- 10 environmental problem signals.
- Existing solutions and limitations.
- Potential impact and economic opportunities.

### R11 — Human Capability

**Scope:** Education, accessibility, assistive technology, productivity, and human learning.

Research questions:
- What limits access to education and essential capabilities?
- Where are assistive technologies insufficient?
- How can people learn complex skills more effectively?
- What prevents individuals from becoming more productive?

Expected output:
- 10 capability gaps.
- Beneficiary analysis.
- Existing solution comparisons.
- Potential accessibility and productivity opportunities.

### R12 — Physical World Experiments

**Scope:** Robotics, sensors, edge intelligence, physical automation, and connected devices.

Research questions:
- What physical tasks remain expensive to automate?
- Where could low-cost sensors improve operations?
- Which manual processes are suitable for robotics?
- What physical-world problems can be addressed with affordable technology?

Expected output:
- 10 automation candidates.
- Hardware and software considerations.
- Technical feasibility and cost assumptions.

## 3. Cross-Division Research

Some of the most valuable opportunities may emerge from combining divisions.

Initial collaboration themes:

| Divisions | Research theme |
|---|---|
| R01 + R04 | Convert customer problems into automated workflows |
| R02 + R03 | Connect knowledge to improve scenario simulation |
| R04 + R05 | Enable autonomous workers to detect and recover failures |
| R06 + R07 | Commercialize trust and verification infrastructure |
| R08 + R10 | Combine resource optimization with environmental benefits |
| R11 + R12 | Affordable physical automation for accessibility |

## 4. Common Research Deliverables

Every division must document:

1. Research question.
2. Problem signals and evidence.
3. Existing products and alternatives.
4. Identified opportunity gaps.
5. Potential beneficiaries and customers.
6. Economic and technical considerations.
7. Risks and unresolved assumptions.
8. Recommended next action.

## 5. Research Assignment Rules

- Every assignment receives a unique research ID.
- Every important finding must have a source.
- Supporting and opposing evidence must be recorded.
- Research outputs must distinguish verified facts from hypotheses.
- Findings may be shared across divisions.
- Duplicate findings should be linked rather than silently discarded.
- Research quantity must never replace evidence quality.

## 6. Initial Operating Philosophy

All 12 divisions are active research areas.

Research can run in parallel, but resources should be allocated according to opportunity quality, evidence confidence, and strategic importance.

No division receives automatic priority merely because its technology is fashionable.

## 7. Success Criteria

A division is successful when it produces useful, traceable research that improves NYR's understanding of real-world problems and opportunities.

**Research broadly. Connect intelligently. Validate selectively.**
