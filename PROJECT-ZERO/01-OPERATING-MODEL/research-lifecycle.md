# NYR Project Zero — Research Lifecycle

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the standard lifecycle used to investigate problems and discover opportunities across all 12 NYR research divisions.

The lifecycle ensures that ideas are investigated systematically rather than moving directly from discovery to implementation.

## 2. Lifecycle Overview

Signal Discovery
    ↓
Problem Extraction
    ↓
Evidence Collection
    ↓
Landscape Investigation
    ↓
Opportunity Formulation
    ↓
Evaluation and Ranking
    ↓
Validation Experiment Design
    ↓
Decision

## 3. Stage 01 — Signal Discovery

Objective: Identify observations that may indicate an emerging problem or opportunity.

Potential sources:
- Research papers and publications.
- Customer reviews and complaints.
- Industry reports.
- Public datasets.
- Technology communities.
- Patents and technical documentation.
- Business and economic trends.
- Government and institutional reports.

Required outputs:
- Unique signal ID.
- Signal description.
- Research division.
- Original source.
- Geography and industry, where relevant.
- Observation date.
- Initial relevance assessment.

Important: A signal is an observation, not proof of a problem.

## 4. Stage 02 — Problem Extraction

Objective: Convert useful signals into clearly described problems.

Investigate:
- Who experiences the problem?
- What exactly is happening?
- Why does the problem exist?
- How frequently does it occur?
- How severe are its consequences?
- What workarounds exist?
- Who is affected geographically and economically?

Required output:
A structured problem statement with affected users, root causes, current workarounds, and evidence references.

## 5. Stage 03 — Evidence Collection

Objective: Establish whether the problem is real and sufficiently important.

Evidence categories:
- Direct observations.
- Customer experiences.
- Quantitative datasets.
- Independent research.
- Industry studies.
- Technical evidence.
- Supporting and contradictory claims.

Requirements:
- Prefer independent sources.
- Record publication and access dates.
- Assess source reliability.
- Separate facts from assumptions.
- Document missing evidence.

Important market or demand claims should ordinarily have at least two independent supporting sources where available.

## 6. Stage 04 — Landscape Investigation

Objective: Understand what already exists.

Investigate:
- Commercial products.
- Open-source projects.
- Competitors.
- Academic research.
- Patents.
- Standards and regulations.
- Failed or abandoned solutions.
- Existing workarounds.

Identify:
- What works?
- What does not work?
- What remains expensive?
- What is inaccessible?
- Which customer segments are underserved?
- Is the apparent opportunity already solved?

Required output:
An existing-solution landscape and documented opportunity gaps.

## 7. Stage 05 — Opportunity Formulation

Objective: Define a possible solution to a sufficiently understood problem.

Document:
- Opportunity title.
- Problem being addressed.
- Proposed solution.
- Target beneficiaries.
- Potential paying customers.
- Value proposition.
- Differentiation.
- Possible business model.
- Technical requirements.
- Key assumptions.
- Major risks.

An opportunity must reference at least one documented problem.

## 8. Stage 06 — Evaluation and Ranking

Objective: Compare opportunities using a consistent evaluation framework.

Evaluate:
- Problem severity.
- Evidence and demand.
- Differentiation and novelty.
- Economic potential.
- Feasibility.
- Global impact.
- Scalability.
- Timing.

Record the score breakdown, evidence confidence, evaluation date, and reviewer.

A high score does not automatically authorize implementation.

## 9. Stage 07 — Validation Experiment Design

Objective: Design the cheapest meaningful experiment capable of testing the most important assumptions.

Every experiment must define:
- Hypothesis.
- Target users or environment.
- Methodology.
- Budget limit.
- Duration.
- Success criteria.
- Failure criteria.
- Measurement approach.
- Ethical and legal considerations.

Prefer experiments that produce measurable evidence over demonstrations that merely look impressive.

## 10. Stage 08 — Decision

Available outcomes:

### Advance
Evidence supports additional investigation or an approved validation activity.

### Hold
The opportunity may be valuable, but timing, resources, or evidence are insufficient.

### Pivot
The original approach needs substantial modification.

### Reject
The opportunity is not worth further investment under current assumptions.

### Monitor
The opportunity is interesting but requires future signals or changing conditions.

Every decision must include a written rationale.

## 11. Research Quality Requirements

Each significant investigation should ordinarily include:
- At least five relevant sources where available.
- At least two independent sources for important market/problem claims where available.
- Existing competitor or alternative analysis.
- A search for contradictory evidence.
- Evidence confidence assessment.
- Known limitations.
- A recommendation.

If these requirements cannot be met, document why.

## 12. Lifecycle Principle

Research is iterative. New evidence can return an opportunity to an earlier stage.

**Never confuse discovery with validation or evaluation with proof.**
