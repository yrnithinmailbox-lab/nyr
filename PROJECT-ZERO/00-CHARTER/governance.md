# NYR Project Zero — Research Governance

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how NYR Project Zero is organized, how research is managed, and how decisions are recorded.

## 2. Organizational Structure

| ID | Division |

| R01 | Opportunity Intelligence |
| R02 | Collective Knowledge |

Divisions may collaborate on cross-disciplinary opportunities.

## 3. Research Lifecycle

Every opportunity should follow this lifecycle:

1. Signal discovery.
2. Problem extraction.
3. Evidence collection.
4. Existing landscape investigation.
5. Opportunity formulation.
6. Evaluation and ranking.
7. Validation experiment design.
8. Decision and documented outcome.

## 4. Research Records

Research records should distinguish:

- Signal: An observation that may indicate a problem or opportunity.
- Problem: A sufficiently described issue affecting identifiable users.
- Opportunity: A potential way to address a verified problem.
- Experiment: A structured test of a hypothesis.
- Decision: A documented choice to advance, hold, pivot, or reject.

## 5. Decision Authority

Research findings and opportunity scores support decisions but do not automatically authorize expenditure or product development.

Significant investment, external commitments, and product implementation require explicit authorization.

A high opportunity score with low evidence confidence is a research hypothesis, not build approval.

## 6. Evidence and Traceability

Important claims must link to their supporting or opposing evidence.

Research records should preserve:
- Source identity and canonical URL.
- Publisher or source owner.
- Publication date, where available.
- Access date.
- Relevant usage terms.
- Evidence strength.
- Verification status.

## 7. Information Classification

### Public

- Research methodology.
- Public research questions.
- Published findings.
- Opportunity evaluation methods.
- Approved reports and experiments.

### Restricted

- Personal or confidential information.
- Credentials and access tokens.
- Sensitive intellectual property.
- Restricted third-party information.
- Information not authorized for public distribution.

Open research does not mean unrestricted disclosure.

## 8. Intellectual Property

Research outputs should have clear ownership and licensing decisions.

Proposed defaults, subject to final review:

- Documentation: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0).
- Original code: MIT or Apache License 2.0.
- Research datasets: License selected only after ownership and redistribution rights are verified.

Third-party content must not be republished merely because it was publicly accessible.

## 9. Research Ethics and Compliance

All research must respect:
- Applicable laws and regulations.
- Privacy and data protection.
- Intellectual property rights.
- Website and API terms.
- Dataset licenses.
- Security and responsible disclosure.

Unknown licensing status must be treated as unresolved, not assumed to permit commercial use.

## 10. Change Management

Changes to research principles, evaluation criteria, source policies, or governance must be documented.

Significant changes should include:
- What changed.
- Why it changed.
- Expected impact.
- Approval or decision reference.

## 11. Initial Operating Constraints

During the initial research foundation phase:

- Prioritize manual research and documented public APIs.
- Do not introduce paid infrastructure without justification.
- Do not build automated scraping infrastructure.
- Do not deploy applications or databases.
- Do not begin product development without separate authorization.

## 12. Governance Principle

**Research must be transparent, evidence-driven, traceable, responsible, and economically disciplined.**
