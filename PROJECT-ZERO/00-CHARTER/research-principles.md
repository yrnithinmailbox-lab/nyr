# NYR Project Zero — Research Principles

These principles govern all research activities across NYR.

## 1. Problem First

Start with a real problem, unmet need, inefficiency, or emerging opportunity.

Do not begin with a technology looking for a reason to exist.

## 2. Evidence Before Assumptions

Distinguish between:
- Observed signals.
- Verified problems.
- Supported market hypotheses.
- Unvalidated assumptions.
- Speculative concepts.

A signal is not proof of a problem. A problem is not proof of demand.

## 3. Investigate Existing Solutions

Before claiming an opportunity is new:
- Search existing products and services.
- Investigate competitors and alternatives.
- Review academic research.
- Search relevant patents.
- Examine abandoned and failed approaches.
- Identify limitations and underserved segments.

Never claim originality without documenting the investigation.

## 4. Global Perspective

Research opportunities across geographies, industries, economic conditions, and user groups.

Do not assume that a problem observed in one market exists everywhere.

## 5. Unconventional Thinking

Investigate ideas that may initially appear unusual, ambitious, or technically challenging.

Speculative concepts are welcome, but must be labelled as hypotheses until supported by evidence.

## 6. Economic Discipline

Investigate:
- Who experiences the problem?
- Who benefits from solving it?
- Who would pay?
- What alternatives exist?
- What would delivery cost?
- Can the solution scale sustainably?

Impact and revenue should both be considered.

## 7. Independent Evidence

Use multiple independent sources for important claims wherever possible.

Seek contradictory evidence, not just evidence supporting an attractive idea.

Community discussions are useful discovery signals, not automatically representative market research.

## 8. Experiment Before Heavy Investment

Prefer inexpensive, falsifiable experiments before committing significant resources.

Define the hypothesis, method, budget, success criteria, and limitations before conducting an experiment.

## 9. Preserve Research Provenance

Record original sources, URLs, publication dates, access dates, evidence quality, and applicable usage restrictions.

Do not remove contradictory evidence or silently discard duplicate observations.

## 10. Responsible Innovation

Respect privacy, security, intellectual property, licensing, platform terms, and applicable laws.

Do not pursue opportunities that depend on unauthorized access, deception, or harmful exploitation.

## 11. Open Knowledge, Responsible Disclosure

Share research methods, findings, and lessons wherever appropriate.

Do not expose confidential information, personal data, credentials, restricted third-party content, or sensitive intellectual property.

## 12. Learn From Failure

A rejected hypothesis or unsuccessful experiment is valuable when it produces documented learning.

Do not hide failures to make research appear successful.

## Guiding Rule

**Curiosity creates hypotheses. Evidence creates confidence. Experiments create knowledge. Execution creates value.**
